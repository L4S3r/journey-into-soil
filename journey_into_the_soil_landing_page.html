Subsurface Threats & Degradation

The Hidden Battlefield in the Soil

Yield loss is rarely an accident of weather alone. We simulate and detect four primary target contaminants that choke roots, starve microbiomes, and trap toxic salts.

ECe > 4.0 dS/m Hazard

1. Root-Zone Salinity & Crust Formation

High evaporation rates and brackish irrigation pull sodium and chloride ions into the top 20cm, exceeding the Maas-Hoffman threshold and suffocating osmotic root uptake.

Maas-Hoffman Yield Threshold: Cotton: 7.7 | Wheat: 6.0 | Berseem: 1.5 dS/m
Mitigation Modeled: Leaching Fraction & Gypsum Replacement
Biological Fatigue

2. Crop-Family Fatigue & Nematode Pressure

Repeating identical crop families (e.g., Nightshades like Tomato/Potato or Monoculture Cotton) multiplies Fusarium wilt and root-knot nematodes in the B-horizon.

Rule Penalty Factor: -25 pts on same botanical family
Mitigation Modeled: Rotational Break with Poaceae/Legumes
Nutrient Exhaustion

3. Nitrogen Leaching vs. Heavy Feeders

Successive seasons of heavy nitrogen-demanding crops (Maize, Sugar cane) without biological fixation drain the organic nitrogen pool and leach nitrates past the root zone into deep gravel.

N-Balance Penalty: -15 pts for N-deficit < -3
Mitigation Modeled: Clover/Berseem Symbiosis (+52 kg N/ha)
Root Resistance Barrier

4. Clay Pan Compaction (Low Ksat)

Heavy tractor traffic and ill-timed flood irrigation create a dense plow pan at 50–90cm. Shallow-rooted crops drown in saturated upper horizons while lower aquifers stay dry.

Hydraulic Conductivity: Clay Ksat reduced by 70%
Mitigation Modeled: Deep Taproot Bio-Drilling (Faba, Alfalfa)
